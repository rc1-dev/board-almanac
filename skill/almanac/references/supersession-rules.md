# Supersession and conflict rules

These rules decide, topic by topic, which statement is current. They are applied in order. When no rule decides, the topic is recorded as a conflict for people to resolve.

Only original documents and recordings are evidence. The previous almanac records earlier conclusions; it is never cited as the source of a rule.

## 1. Same topic and same subject first

Two statements can only replace or contradict each other if they are about the same subject and the same scope.

- Different subjects do not conflict. "Quiet hours on Friday and Saturday nights" does not replace "quiet hours on other nights".
- Different periods do not conflict. A 2025 fee and a 2026 fee are a history, not a contradiction.
- Different units, rooms, seasons or groups do not conflict unless a document says the rule applies to all of them.
- Partly overlapping scopes: when a later adopted statement covers only part of an earlier rule's scope, for example Friday and Saturday nights within a rule for every night, it replaces the earlier rule only for the overlap. Record the earlier rule as replaced for the overlap and still current for the rest of its scope, subject to the authority and effective-date rules below. If the two scopes cannot be compared clearly, record a conflict instead.

## 2. Authority ranking

When statements about the same subject and scope disagree, the higher-ranked source type wins only where the lower one has no authority to change it. Rank a statement by the provision it comes from, not by the file it sits in: one document can hold both bylaws, which only an owner vote can amend, and house rules, which the board can amend by resolution.

1. Bylaws and declaration provisions (changed only by the owner vote the bylaws require)
2. House rules, adopted board resolutions in signed or approved minutes, and signed contracts. A board resolution replaces a house rule when the governing documents give the board that power, as a board majority does in the test archive.
3. Board notices to residents
4. Agendas, newsletters and emails
5. Meeting recording transcripts (always provisional)

A notice that restates an adopted resolution is evidence for it. A notice that disagrees with an adopted resolution does not replace it: record a conflict unless a later document explains the difference.

## 3. Later adopted decisions replace earlier ones

Within the same rank, a later adopted decision replaces an earlier one on the same subject when either:

- it states the new rule for the same subject and scope, or
- it explicitly amends, corrects, replaces or repeals the earlier rule.

Use the document date and effective date, never the file modification date. The replaced rule moves to Superseded with its date range.

## 4. Corrections

If a later adopted document says an earlier statement was an error (for example, "the February notice stating $230 was in error; the adopted fee is $225"), record the corrected value as current for that period, keep the erroneous statement in Superseded marked "corrected", and close any conflict it caused.

## 5. Failed and proposed motions

A failed motion or an agenda proposal never changes a rule. Record it under Superseded only if it helps the reader, marked "proposed, not adopted" or "failed".

## 6. Effective dates

If a decision has a future effective date, the current rule stays until that date. Show both: the current rule, and the adopted change under Scheduled with its effective date. Once that date has passed, the scheduled change is the current rule, even if no document has changed since the compile. Ask, Lint and Status check this against today's date.

## 7. Conflicts

Record a conflict when two statements about the same subject and scope disagree and none of rules 2 to 6 decides. A conflict entry quotes both statements with file and date. The At a glance status becomes `conflict`. Never pick a winner by majority, recency of file date or plausibility.

## 8. Things the Skill does not judge

- Whether a vote met a quorum or the bylaws threshold. If the documents state a threshold and a vote, record both; do not rule on validity.
- Whether a contract notice was legally effective. Record the deadline and the date the documents show for the action, and flag a late or missing action in Open items.
